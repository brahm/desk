An office suite made for GNOME

Desk is a free office suite of three apps designed to work as one, with each other and with the GNOME desktop. They share one document model, one window and one way of doing things: a table, a chart or a block of text behaves the same in all of them, and everything looks and feels like the rest of your desktop. Each app is simple and focused on its job, without giving up the power you need for real work.

  • Desk Writer

    Word processing and page layout in one app, from a school paper to the bakery's flyer: styles, sections and columns, footnotes, tables of contents, and pictures and shapes that the text flows around.

  • Desk Tables

    Spreadsheets laid out like a page: several free-floating tables and charts on each sheet, formulas you can read, and good-looking charts. It is for organizing and calculating, not a business intelligence tool or a database.

  • Desk Slides

    Presentations with themes, transitions and builds that make the audience forget to check their phones, and a presenter display with your notes and the next slide.

These are mockups of the window the three apps will share, drawn with the real GTK 4 and Libadwaita widgets at 1280 × 720, the smallest screen Desk is made for.

Why Desk

Desk did not come from a lack of office suites on Linux. LibreOffice and ONLYOFFICE do that job very well. But neither of them follows the GNOME Human Interface Guidelines, and neither feels at home on the GNOME desktop, which is where I spend my days.

There is also a matter of taste. I prefer simple tools, and simple does not mean weak. So instead of following Microsoft 365, the direction LibreOffice tends to take these days (even though it comes from StarOffice, which was quite original in its time), I looked the other way: to Apple's iWork.

I have always been a fan of iWork '09, the one from before online collaboration, AI and subscriptions. I used it every day, and I never quite got over the move from 32 to 64 bits, which broke compatibility with my files and took away several unique and very useful features. To be fair, today's Pages, Keynote and Numbers have brought many of them back, some of them behind a paywall.

Desk is not a clone of iWork. I use iWork as a yardstick for what a simple and capable office suite can do, not as a blueprint to copy. The goal is an office suite with simpler, focused apps, perfectly integrated with GNOME. Desk Tables does not try to be a business intelligence tool or a database, for example: it is a spreadsheet, and a good one.

Some things you can expect:

  • Three apps that share one document model, one window and one Inspector, so what you learn in one works in the others.
  • Built with Rust, GTK 4 and Libadwaita, following the GNOME Human Interface Guidelines, with documents in tabs and automatic saving with versions.
  • Documents are saved as OpenDocument (ODF). Your iWork files, old and new, and your Microsoft Office files will open too.
  • No AI, no online account and no subscription needed for anything.
  • And if the community asks really nicely, we will port it to Plasma. :)

How Desk is built

A few decisions shape all of Desk. They are recorded, with their reasons, in the architecture and decisions pages of the repository.

  • The engine is apart from the interfaceThe document model, text shaping, pagination, rendering, formulas, charts and file formats are Rust libraries with no GTK at all. Only the interface library and the three apps use GTK 4 and Libadwaita, and the CI checks it on every change. That is how desk-convert already turns documents into PDF from the command line, and how other projects can reuse the engine.
  • File format libraries anyone can useThe OpenDocument, iWork and formula libraries depend on no other part of Desk and are licensed under MPL-2.0, so that other programs, free or not, can read and write the same files. The apps are GPL-3.0-or-later.
  • One document model, one way to change itThe three apps share one model, so an object copied from one to another stays the same object. Every change is an operation with an inverse, which gives undo for free, and the same document always gives the same pages on screen, in PDF and in the tests.
  • OpenDocument firstDocuments are standard ODF. Where ODF has no place for a feature, Desk adds a documented extension that other programs can safely ignore. Files of iWork and Microsoft Office are read by import filters, which tell you what they could not keep.
  • Native GNOME, nothing borrowedA Libadwaita widget wherever one exists. Where none does, Desk builds one that follows the GNOME guidelines and reviews it with each new Libadwaita. Spelling, printing, fonts, file dialogs and the screen reader come from the platform.
  • Flatpak on GNOME 51, and nothing onlineDesk ships as a Flatpak on the GNOME 51 runtime, with GTK 4.24 and Libadwaita 1.10. It has no server and no account: everything works offline.
  • Measured, not guessedEvery feature is a row of an inventory with its phase and tests, and the output is compared with documents made by iWork itself. Interface tests drive the apps through the accessibility tree, the way a screen reader does.

Where Desk is today

Desk Writer came first, because the text engine is the hardest part and the other two apps depend on it. It already has its text engine, styles and document structure, spelling and grammar, pictures, shapes and media, tables and charts, and it opens Pages '09 documents with the layout of Pages. It is in beta, in the old window. Desk Tables and Desk Slides have their window and engines for tables, formulas, charts and animation, which the apps do not use yet.

In November 2026 I set new goals for the project: Desk now measures itself against the features of the current Pages, Numbers and Keynote, keeping what iWork '09 did and the newer versions lost, and leaving out what needs AI, an online service or a payment. Desk is built by me together with Claude, Anthropic's AI, with the rigor expected of any team: every feature is a row in an inventory, every change goes through review and a CI, and the output is compared with documents made by iWork itself. The progress dashboard (in Portuguese) shows each feature, phase and forecast, updated with every change.

What comes next

After a common base, the three apps can move forward side by side. Counting them one after the other, the forecast is feature parity near the end of January 2027. It is an estimate, not a promise, and it is corrected at the end of each phase.

  1. Desk Writer, first round beta

    Text engine, styles, document structure, writing tools, objects, tables and charts, and opening Pages '09 documents.

  2. New goals now

    The goals, decisions, feature inventory, chosen window and roadmap that guide everything below.

  3. Common base

    The shared window in the three apps, documents in tabs, automatic saving with versions, and the reference documents remade on the current iWork.

  4. Desk Writer

    The behaviour of the current Pages, comments and change tracking, page templates and mail merge, new objects and charts, Word and Pages files, printing and export.

  5. Desk Tables

    Its own files, cells and formulas, sorting, filters, categories and pivot tables, charts, and Excel and Numbers files.

  6. Desk Slides

    Themes and masters, text and media, transitions and builds, presenting with the presenter display, and PowerPoint and Keynote files.

  7. Across the apps, and version 1.0

    Charts and tables linked between documents that update when the source changes, copy and paste among the apps, and the final parity report.

Install

Desk Writer is in beta, as a Flatpak from Desk's own repository. Add the repository and install the app:

flatpak remote-add --if-not-exists desk https://brahm.github.io/desk/desk.flatpakrepo
flatpak install desk br.com.brahm.Desk.Writer//beta

Or open desk-writer-beta.flatpakref to install it with GNOME Software. It is a test version: keep a copy of the documents you open with it. For the version built every night from the latest code, use //nightly or desk-writer-nightly.flatpakref. You can also build Desk from the source code.

Desk needs a screen of at least 1280 × 720 pixels, after display scaling: a desktop, a laptop or a tablet held sideways. Phones and smaller screens are not supported. The apps still open on them, with a warning that parts of the window may not fit.

Get involved

Desk is free software and still young, so every bit of help counts. We are one person and one AI: please be patient with us, and thank you in advance.

  • Report an issueFound a bug? Tell us what happened and how to repeat it.
  • Request a featureMissing something you use every day? Just ask.
  • Send old iWork documentsPages, Numbers and Keynote files, especially from iWork '09, help us test compatibility.
  • Contribute codeThe code is on GitHub, in Rust, with CI running every check before each merge.
  • TranslateThe interface is in English and Brazilian Portuguese. Add your language with a PO editor such as Gtranslator or Poedit.
  • Follow the progressThe dashboard shows every feature, phase, open item and the forecast, updated with each change. It is in Portuguese.
