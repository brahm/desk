## ODF

Comments are the standard `office:annotation`, and a comment on a range
ends at `office:annotation-end`; a comment on an object or a cell is the
annotation of that frame or cell. Insertions, deletions and the format
changes that ODF can name are the standard `text:tracked-changes`, with
`text:changed-region`, `text:insertion`, `text:deletion` and
`text:format-change`, each with its `dc:creator` and `dc:date`. Three
things have no place in ODF 1.3 and are extensions, each defined in
[odf-extensions.md](../odf-extensions.md) in the pull request that adds
it, with the fallback that file already names: the replies of a thread
(flat annotations, in order, as a reader without it sees them; the
threading that LibreOffice writes is read and written where it says the
same), and the changes to objects and cells. A highlight is an
annotation without text, in the colour of its author, as in Pages 15.4,
and needs nothing more. Pausing, the markup view and the colour of each
author are settings of the document.

## Order of the pull requests

Small pull requests, each reviewed on its own. The Mac is used in one
session at the start, announced to the owner first, and in one at the
end.

1. This plan.
2. Session on the Mac: what Pages 15.4 does for each row marked "Before",
   with what Pages '09 tracked, seen on its virtual machine, and the
   check of the rows marked "Check". From it, `REV-01` to `REV-08`
   rewritten for 15.4 with the phase W2, and a row for the review
   highlight. Done on 2026-11-22: what it saw is in the column "Mac"
   above, and the highlight is `PXV-19`.
3. The model of comments: anchors, threads, authors and dates, in the
   undo of the document; read and written as ODF, ranges included, and
   kept from ODT files instead of dropped. Done: the text of the body
   carries the comments it is under, which may overlap and span
   paragraphs, typed text inside a comment joins it, and a comment whose
   text is all deleted goes with it. ODT keeps them as annotations with
   their ends, and the replies with `desk:parent-name`; the replies that
   LibreOffice writes with `loext:parent-name` join their comment, and a
   comment at a point, as other programs write one, is on the word
   there. The comments of notes, headers, footers, text boxes and shapes
   are still listed as left out when a file opens, and so are those of
   Pages '09 files; steps 4 and 5 bring them.
4. Comments in the window: the Comment button and the menu, the bubbles
   in the margin, replies and delete, the Comments & Changes pane of the
   sidebar, and the comments of Pages '09 files (W149), in three pull
   requests. The first is done: Comment, on the selection or the word at
   the cursor, in the header bar, the Insert menu and Ctrl+Shift+K; the
   Comments page of the sidebar with a card for each comment, written,
   answered, edited and deleted in place; the text under comments marked
   on the page; Next and Previous Comment and the arrows of the window.
   The second is done too: the mark of each comment in the left margin,
   whose click shows the comment in a bubble at its text, with the
   buttons to the comment before and after; Next and Previous Comment
   show it there, and a new comment is written there while the Comments
   page is not showing. The third is done as well: the comments of the
   body text of Pages '09 files open on their text, with what they say,
   and a comment at the cursor stays on the space it was made on, as
   Pages '09 shows it; those files have no author or date for them.
   Their comments on shapes, text boxes and cells are still listed as
   left out, until step 5.
5. Comments on objects and cells, the comment of a cell joining the
   threads, and comments in notes, headers, footers, text boxes and
   shapes (W149), in four pull requests. The first is done: the model
   keeps a comment on the text of a note, a header, a footer, a text box
   or a shape as on the body, changed in a copy whose body is that text,
   lists every comment of the document with the text it is in, and
   deletes one from anywhere; ODT reads and writes them as the
   annotations of that text. The comments of the paragraphs of tables
   and of the shapes of groups are still listed as left out. The second
   is done too: Comment writes one in the header, footer, note, text box
   or shape being edited; their text is marked, with its square in the
   margin; the Comments page lists every comment by page, as Pages 15.4
   does, and Next and Previous Comment go through them in that order,
   into the text each is in. The third is done as well: a comment on an
   object itself, written with the object selected, kept on it in the
   model and in ODT, where it is a `desk:annotation` of the object since
   ODF has annotations only in text and cells; its mark is at the top
   left corner of the object, and going to it selects the object; the
   comments of Pages '09 files on shapes open on them. A fourth pull
   request brings the comments on cells, the comment of a cell joining
   the threads. The fourth is done too: the comment of a cell is a
   comment of the document, with its replies, which the cell carries as
   it moves, written with the cell selected or in the Table pane, its
   mark the corner of the cell, and in ODT the annotation of the cell.
   Those of Pages '09 files wait for the tables of those files, which
   Desk does not read yet: a fifth pull request reads them, with the
   comments of their cells (B-048), as the owner chose on 2026-11-23.
   It is done: the tables in the text of Pages '09 files open with their
   grid, sizes, headers, footers, what their cells hold, formulas and
   merged cells, and the comments of their cells; they look as new
   tables of Desk do, and a sixth pull request brings their styles
   (B-049).
